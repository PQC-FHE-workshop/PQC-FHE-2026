About the Workshop

The global transition to next-generation cryptography necessitates a comprehensive and rigorous exploration of diverse cryptographic primitives. The PQC-FHE 2026 workshop is dedicated to the algorithmic construction, parameter optimization, and security analysis of two critical domains: Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) and Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE). For PQC, the workshop will explore concrete design and cryptanalysis across diverse paradigms (including lattice-based, isogeny-based, code-based, and multivariate schemes). For FHE, the event will delve into the algorithm design and practical deployment of advanced schemes (e.g., BGV, BFV, CKKS, TFHE) and crucial optimization techniques.

With the historic milestones in the NIST standardisation of diverse PQC algorithms and the ongoing ISO/IEC 28033 standardisation efforts for FHE, a dedicated forum to discuss concrete design choices, cryptanalysis, and standardisation roadmaps for both PQC and FHE is highly timely. Hosting this event on Monday, December 7, at Hong Kong Polytechnic University will attract significant interest from the Asiacrypt community, providing a vital platform for algorithm designers, cryptanalysts, and industry implementers to address the design and deployment challenges of post-quantum and privacy-preserving cryptography.

Format

The event will be formatted as a workshop combining invited talks from leading experts and submitted presentations from the community. The workshop will be held as a whole-day event. Standard conference room setup with a projector, screen, and basic internet access will suffice. No specialized technical equipment is requested beyond the local organization's provisions.

Estimated Number of Attendees

Total estimated attendees: 50 – 70. Expected student attendees: 20 – 30.

Potential Attendees

The workshop expects to attract a diverse yet highly focused group of researchers, engineers, and standardisation experts whose work aligns with the following core research directions:

  • Post-Quantum Cryptographic Design and Cryptanalysis: Researchers specializing in the theoretical construction, parameter optimization, and security evaluation of diverse PQC paradigms.
  • Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) Optimization: Cryptographers and applied mathematicians focused on the algorithmic refinement, bootstrapping efficiency, and implementation of advanced homomorphic schemes.
  • Hardware-Software Co-Design for Cryptography: Engineers and computer architects working on low-latency cryptographic hardware accelerators.
  • Cryptographic Standardization and Open-Source Engineering: Core contributors and working group members engaged in benchmarking, developing global deployment guidelines, and building robust open-source cryptographic ecosystems.

Organizers

Xianhui Lu

Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences (IIE, CAS) luxianhui@iie.ac.cn

Xianhui Lu is a Researcher and Doctoral Advisor at IIE, CAS. His primary research interests encompass the provable security theory of cryptographic algorithms, Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), and 6G cryptography. He led the design of the post-quantum cryptographic algorithm LAC. As an expert for the ISO/IEC, he actively participates in the international standardization efforts for both PQC and Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE). Furthermore, he leads a 6G cryptography research team. He has published numerous academic papers in top-tier cryptographic conferences, such as EUROCRYPT, ASIACRYPT, and PKC.

Ruida Wang

Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences (IIE, CAS) wangruida@iie.ac.cn

Ruida Wang is a Research Associate at IIE, CAS. His primary research interests focus on cryptography, with a particular emphasis on the theory, efficient implementation, and practical application of Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE). He actively participates in international cryptographic standardization efforts, and contributes to open-source cryptographic ecosystems. He has published a series of impactful academic papers in top-tier cryptography and security conferences, including EUROCRYPT and CCS.
